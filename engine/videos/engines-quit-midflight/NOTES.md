# NOTES: engines-quit-midflight (daily run, 2026-11-25)

Shared code was not edited. For the owner and whoever maintains the manual:

1. **Topic.** Today's trend file had no fresh everyday "what if you" row that passes the four tests (adverts,
   GTA 6, film and game rows; the one physical row is our own first episode), so the topic is a tier A entry of the
   topic bank outside the clusters of the last two episodes (space-you, five-seconds). Thanksgiving travel is in
   today's trending searches, which is the week for a plane story.
2. **The headline number.** "Fifteen miles ahead for each mile it sinks" is an assumed, cautious glide ratio inside
   the published range (cruise L/D of airliners 15 to 19; real engine-out glides about 12 to 17). It gives 108
   miles and 18.4 minutes from 38,000 ft; the narration says "over a hundred miles" and "almost twenty minutes",
   the description gives the 86 to 122 mile range. The glide speed (230 knots) only enters the time.
3. **The real glide** (24 August 2001, 75 miles to the Azores, 306 on board, nobody died) is told without names.
   18 people were hurt during the evacuation; the narration says "they all lived", the description says both.
4. **"Gliding home his whole life"** is the joke with a licence: a normal descent is flown at or near idle thrust
   from about 130 miles out, which is close to a glide, not one. SCRIPT.md and the description say so.
5. **Sources.** Four Wikipedia pages and the U.S. Access Board page were opened; the accident report on the FAA
   site refused (HTTP 403) and the AOPA and NASA items were seen as search results only. SCRIPT.md lists which.
6. **Voice.** The first draft ran 73 s and the second 3.33 words/s; short sentences with many full stops cost
   more than their words. The final take is 67.0 s at 3.52 words/s (the film is 67.5 s, near the upper limit).
   "Gus's" was avoided as in earlier runs.
7. **Seat-back screen.** Characters are cards that face the lens, so a screen and its viewer cannot be in one
   frame. The movie is shown from Gus's point of view: one card (seat back, screen, tray table) that fills the
   frame 1.3 m in front of the lens in the cabin place; his face is drawn into the black glass as a reflection.
   The lens cannot push in on a card that follows it, so the card creeps closer instead (`povDist`).
8. **Pointing hand.** A single raised finger on a fist read as a rude gesture on the first contact sheet; the hand
   now has three curled fingers, a thumb and leans 17 degrees. Worth remembering for any poke at a screen.
9. **Card order.** Two cards 8 cm apart in depth did not sort reliably (the bike in front of Gus's legs); half a
   metre did.
10. **Diagrams.** The triangle of shot 15 is true to scale (1 to 15). The two wedges of shot 16 (glide and
    wheelchair ramp) are both drawn four times steeper, or one degree of difference would not show on a phone.
    The bike hill is 20 degrees and tips down to the true 3.8 degrees in shot 14.
11. **Cartoon licence.** The engine coughs smoke in the hook (real engines out of fuel just stop). The ram air
    turbine is drawn under the belly and larger than life; on some types it sits elsewhere and the Boeing 737
    has none. The island runway is drawn on the island's back.
