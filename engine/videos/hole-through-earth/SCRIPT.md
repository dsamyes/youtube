# "What If You Jumped Into a Hole Through the Earth?" — original script v1 (2026-11-13)
Format: cartoon-3d-short skill (~64 s, 3.7 words/s, Kokoro am_michael @1.2). Our own characters and story.
Recurring character: **Gus** (the If Engine's volunteer): pyjamas, slippers, glass fishbowl helmet.

Facts (every number is computed in `calc_video.py` → `calc.json`; model: airless, frictionless tunnel through the
centre of a non-rotating Earth, real density profile PREM):
- One-way trip 38 min 11 s (PREM; matches Klotz 2015, Am. J. Phys. 83, 231). Textbook uniform Earth: 42 min 12 s.
- Round trip 76 min. He passes the centre after 19 min at 9.92 km/s = 35,700 km/h = 22,200 mph.
- He reaches 1 km/s (a fast rifle bullet, ~0.9-1.0 km/s) after 102 s, so "two minutes in, he's outrunning a bullet".
- Centre of the Earth ~5,400-6,000 C; the Sun's surface ~5,500 C (Anzellini et al. 2013, Science 340, 464; NASA Sun fact sheet).
- Gravity inside the Earth always points to the centre, so past the centre it decelerates him; with no losses he
  arrives at the far surface at exactly zero speed, then falls back: an oscillation, like a swing.
- For most places on land the opposite point of the globe is ocean (only ~15 % of land is opposite other land).

Script (17 lines):
1. If you jumped into a hole through the middle of the Earth, you would never hit the bottom.
2. It's Sunday morning, and Gus has just dug a hole in his garden, straight through the planet.
3. His neighbor says he'll burn to a crisp.
4. His wife says he'll go splat on the other side.
5. Gus says he did the math, and jumps.
6. He's pumped out all the air, so nothing slows him down.
7. Two minutes in, he's outrunning a bullet.
8. Nineteen minutes in, he shoots through the center of the Earth at twenty-two thousand miles an hour.
9. The walls are as hot as the surface of the Sun. It's a very good tunnel.
10. But wait. Why doesn't he go splat?
11. You see, gravity only pulls Gus toward the middle. So the moment he passes it, the pull that sped him up starts slowing him down.
12. Ever pushed a kid on a swing? All the speed it gains going down, it spends going back up.
13. So thirty-eight minutes after he jumped, Gus pops up in the middle of the ocean, on the far side of the world, and stops.
14. Luckily, he stops for exactly one moment.
15. Unluckily, he stops for exactly one moment.
16. Then down he goes again, and thirty-eight minutes later, he's back in his own garden.
17. So Gus never did hit the bottom. He just comes home for one second, every seventy-six minutes.
