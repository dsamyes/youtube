# NOTES: pool-100-feet-deep (daily run, 2026-11-28)

Shared code was not edited. For the owner and whoever maintains the manual:

1. **Topic.** Taken from the trend file, not the bank: the week's strongest real "what if" row is a backyard pool
   dug far too deep (111k-subscriber channel, 4.1M views/day) and the digging theme also pulls for a
   1,500-subscriber channel. Theme only: our question is what the bottom of such a pool is like, with our own
   number (100 ft), story and structure.
2. **Safety.** Nobody goes under water. Only a beach ball on a rope does; Gus stays at the rim and ends on the top
   step because he cannot swim. The description adds one line: never dive deep on a held breath, never swim alone.
   The sinking of breath-hold divers (the same squeeze on the air in the chest) is deliberately left out.
3. **Model.** 16 x 32 ft pool, fresh water (34 ft per atmosphere, not the 33 ft of sea water), 15-inch beach ball,
   Boyle's law. "Half" and "a quarter" are volumes; the basketball is the honest width (63 %). A real vinyl ball
   crumples instead of shrinking evenly, so it is drawn wrinkled inside a dashed ring of its first size.
4. **Cartoon licence.** The pool is drawn about 10 x 8 ft, the depth marks are compressed (0.12 m of wall per foot),
   the descent takes seconds, and the hundred-pound barbell is lowered by hand.
5. **Places.** garden (kit town, with the pool added through `dress`), "deep" (this video's under-water place:
   a scrolling tiled wall and floor, unlit), and the open sky of plane-door-midflight used twice ("street" with
   its ground for the dump trucks, "board" without for the balance scale).
